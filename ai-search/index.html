02Problem statement

Every new location meant more questions for HQ

Franchises run on documentation: long libraries of SOPs, guidelines and training material. Every new location brings admins and staff who have to learn all of it, and they have questions. With a few locations, HQ keeps up. With hundreds, it can't.

The questions come in over Delightree chat, WhatsApp and email, and most are simple. The answer is already in the documentation, buried in long SOPs.

HQHQ inbox0
Keeping up

The goal

Let anyone at a new location talk to the knowledge base, not HQ.

They learn how their brand operates, and HQ gets back to growing the business.

On paper, Delightree had conversational search.

But it was confusing and limited

04Solutions

01

Combined search and AI search

One search bar, with placeholder text that says it does both. Type a keyword to find a document, or type a question to start an AI search.

0.0s

Design decision

Where should people start talking to AI?

A chat entry point

Pros

A familiar pattern from ChatGPT and Claude, used by millions of people.

Cons

Makes a complex product with many workflows look like a chatbot.
Inside the existing search Chosen

Pros

Search is where people already go to find things.
AI search doesn't become the hero in a complex B2B SaaS tool.

Cons

Past conversations get harder to find.

02

Answers open in a side panel

On web the answer opens in a side panel over the current screen. On mobile it becomes a full screen.

Design decision

Where should AI search live?

A full page

Pros

Room to show charts and data alongside answers.

Cons

Takes people away from the screen they were working on.
A side panel Chosen

Pros

Opens over whatever you're working on, so nobody loses their place.
Can grow into an AI assistant on every screen as the product becomes AI native.

Cons

Less room for long answers, charts and data.

03

Users can keep the conversation going

Every answer ends with suggested follow-ups, so people can dig deeper instead of starting over. When a thread gets long, a nudge suggests starting fresh, because answers get slower and less accurate.

04

Users can read the actual document behind every answer

Every answer lists the documents it came from. Users can open any source to fact-check the response, then keep reading to finish their research.

05

Users choose where answers come from

Answers come from the brand's own resources by default, with HQ and location knowledge bases answering in separate sections. When users need product help, they switch to the Delightree guide.

06

Users find past conversations where they already look

Recent AI searches sits as a tab next to recent searches, so users can pick up any past conversation from the search bar.

Design decision

Where should past conversations go?

A dedicated history section

Pros

A familiar pattern from ChatGPT and Claude, easy to navigate.

Cons

One more place to learn in an already complex product.
Recent searchesRecent AI searches
A tab in recent searches Chosen

Pros

Sits where people already look, in a pattern they know.
Shorter development time.

Cons

Harder to browse a long history.

07

Speak instead of typing

Users tap the mic, speak, and check the text before sending it. Frontline staff often have their hands full.

08

Feedback on every answer

Users can copy, regenerate or rate any answer. A thumbs down asks for a reason, so the team can see which answers miss.

06Learnings and future iterations

Learnings

  1. A familiar pattern can make your product look like something elseThe chat entry point is everywhere, and people are comfortable with it. On a complex B2B tool, it made the whole product look like a chatbot, which was wrong for our positioning.

Future iterations
